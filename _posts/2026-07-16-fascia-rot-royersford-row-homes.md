Royersford's historic downtown is full of row homes and twins built in the early to mid-1900s. These homes are beautiful — and their narrow lot widths, shallow roof overhangs, and shared walls create gutter challenges that newer suburban construction simply doesn't have.

### Why Row Homes Are Especially Vulnerable

In a typical Royersford row home in the 19468 zip code, the front and rear gutters are short — often 15 to 20 feet each — with minimal slope to the downspout. Any debris accumulation flattens what little pitch exists. Water ponds at the back of the gutter run, sits against the fascia board around the clock, and begins the process of rot.

Once fascia rot sets in, the gutter has nothing solid to anchor to. Screws pull out, the gutter begins to tilt inward, and every rain sends overflow directly against the foundation.

### The Shared Wall Problem

In attached row homes, a single overflowing gutter on one unit can send water toward the shared wall — where it seeps into the basement of the neighboring unit as well. We've seen this situation create disputes between neighbors that were entirely caused by one failing gutter.

### What We Do for Royersford Row Homes

Every installation in a historic Royersford home includes a fascia inspection before any new gutter is hung. If the wood is compromised, we replace it before installation. The new seamless gutter is then set to the correct pitch — not the pitch of the old one, which may have settled over decades — ensuring proper drainage for years to come.
