There is a reason Gilbertsville homeowners call me more in October than any other time of year. The mature white and red oak trees that shade most of 19525 don't drop their leaves until very late in the season — often into December — and when they do, they come down fast and heavy.

Oak leaves are particular troublemakers for gutters. They're large, dense, and they mat together when wet, forming a nearly solid plug that blocks water flow instantly.

### The Double Season Problem

Oak-heavy properties in Gilbertsville often need two cleanings in fall alone. The first wave of lighter debris — maple seeds and birch leaves — arrives in October. Then the oaks let go in November and early December.

If you only clean once in November after the oaks drop, you're starting winter with already-saturated leaves that freeze solid in the gutter. That creates ice dams.

### The Micro-Mesh Solution

The properties that benefit most from micro-mesh gutter guards are exactly these: heavy tree coverage, late-dropping oaks, and a homeowner who doesn't want to think about it twice a season. 

Micro-mesh with a 50-micron stainless steel screen keeps even the smallest debris on top of the guard, where it dries and blows away. Water passes through freely. Cleaning frequency drops dramatically.

For homes with significant tree coverage, we strongly recommend the combination of 6-inch seamless gutters and micro-mesh guards. The extra capacity handles the volume during heavy rain, and the guards keep the debris out of the system entirely.
