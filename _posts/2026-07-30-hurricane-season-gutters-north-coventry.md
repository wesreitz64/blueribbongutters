People don't think of Chester County as hurricane territory — and it isn't. But the remnants of Atlantic hurricanes and tropical storms make their way inland every August and September, and when they do, they dump 3 to 5 inches of rain in 24 hours on North Coventry Township.

Your gutters were not designed for that. Most weren't, anyway.

### What Happens When Volume Exceeds Capacity

The national standard for gutter sizing assumes a 100-year storm design — but the calculation also assumes gutters are clean and unobstructed. A gutter that is 30% full of leaves and maple seeds (very common in the 19428 zip code by August) has 30% less capacity.

When a tropical storm rolls through and your gutters are partially blocked, they overflow — usually from the front run first. That water follows the foundation wall down, pools at the base, and if your basement windows are at or near grade level, comes in.

### What We Recommend for North Coventry Homes

Before late summer, have your gutters cleaned and inspected. If your home has large roof planes — common in the newer developments off Route 23 — consider upgrading to 6-inch gutters with micro-mesh guards. The guards keep debris out, so when the big storm hits in September, your gutters are at full capacity.
